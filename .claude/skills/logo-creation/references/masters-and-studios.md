# How the masters and studios work

Research compiled September 2026 from the designers' own writing, their studios' sites, museums and the
design press (sources under each entry). Use it to choose a direction and to explain a recommendation.
Items the sources did not settle are marked *unverified*.

Contents: the shared method · Rams · Vignelli · Rand · Müller-Brockmann · Aicher · MUJI (Tanaka, Hara) ·
Chermayeff & Geismar & Haviv · Pentagram (Bierut) · Experimental Jetset · Build · Spin · OK-RM · Norm · Order

## The shared method

| Step | Who does it that way | In this skill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research the business first | Rams (understand the problem), Aicher (Ulm research method), Order (archives) | Brief, `site_palette.py` |
| Reduce to one idea | Rand, Spin, OK-RM | The one-sentence brief |
| Explore many, by hand and eye | Chermayeff & Geismar & Haviv (dozens of concepts), Spin (physical making) | `contact_sheet.py` |
| Constrain: one grid, one typeface, few colours | Vignelli, Müller-Brockmann, Aicher, Norm | Step 2 |
| Test small, one colour, from memory, every size | Chermayeff & Geismar & Haviv, Bierut | `proof.py` |
| Write the rules, repeat them exactly | Aicher, Vignelli, Experimental Jetset, Order | README from the template |
| Present one confident answer | Rand | Step 6 |

## Masters

### Dieter Rams (Germany, b. 1932)

- **Work:** head of design at Braun, 1961-1995; designer for Vitsœ since 1959. SK4 radio-phonograph with Hans Gugelot (1956), 606 Universal Shelving System (1960).
- **How he works:** a small in-house team (rarely more than ten), with Rams reviewing every product. Start from the problem and the function, remove everything that is not essential, test with sketches and physical models. One set of details is reused across a whole product range, in neutral colours, with no ornament.
- **In his words:** "Less, but better – because it concentrates on the essential aspects, and the products are not burdened with non-essentials." (Vitsœ, *Good design*)
- **For a logo:** every element must earn its place; repeat the same details everywhere; design it to last.
- **Sources:** vitsoe.com/us/about/good-design · vitsoe.com/us/about/dieter-rams · designboom.com/interviews/dieter-rams-designboom-interview/

### Massimo Vignelli (1931-2014) and Lella Vignelli (1934-2016)

- **Work:** Unimark International's New York office (1966), Vignelli Associates (1971). American Airlines identity (1967), New York subway map and signage (1972), the National Park Service Unigrid system (1977).
- **How they worked:** a fixed grid and a self-imposed shortlist of typefaces (Helvetica, Garamond, Bodoni, Century Expanded, Futura...) applied from logo to signs to maps. The subway map cut geography to straight lines at 45 and 90 degrees, keeping only what a rider needs. Lella ran the practice and its interiors and products, so objects and graphics shared one system.
- **In their words:** "If you can design one thing, you can design everything." (*Design Is One*, 2004)
- **For a logo:** consistency is the design. Choose one typeface and a few rules, then never break them.
- **Sources:** nytransitmuseum.org/vignelli/ · en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massimo_Vignelli · en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lella_Vignelli

### Paul Rand (1914-1996)

- **Work:** IBM (1956, striped version 1972), Westinghouse (1960), UPS (1961), ABC (1962), NeXT (1986). Taught at Yale.
- **How he worked:** did most of the design himself. Presented one resolved solution, not options, argued in a bound booklet of research and sketches, and dealt directly with the client's leadership. Each mark is one geometric idea that works small and in one colour, with wit instead of decoration.
- **In his words:** "A logo derives its meaning from the quality of the thing it symbolizes, not the other way around." (*Logos, Flags, and Escutcheons*, 1991)
- **For a logo:** identify, don't describe; reduce to one idea; present one confident answer with its reasoning.
- **Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Rand · eyeondesign.aiga.org/everything-is-design-celebrates-the-universal-reach-of-paul-rand/

### Josef Müller-Brockmann (Switzerland, 1914-1996)

- **Work:** Tonhalle Zürich concert posters (Beethoven 1955, Musica Viva 1958), *Der Film* and *Weniger Lärm* (1960); the book *Grid Systems in Graphic Design* (1981); design consultant to IBM Europe (1967-88).
- **How he worked:** dropped illustration around 1950 for a mathematical method: every poster built on a geometric grid, one grotesque typeface (Akzidenz-Grotesk), few colours. He then wrote the method down so others could apply it the same way.
- **In his words:** "The fewer the differences in the size of the illustrations, the quieter the impression created by the design." (*Grid Systems in Graphic Design*)
- **For a logo:** build the mark on a module (here: its own x-height) so it reproduces identically everywhere.
- **Sources:** moma.org/collection/artists/4154 · en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Müller-Brockmann · neugraphic.com/muller-brockmann/muller-brockmann-text2.html

### Otl Aicher (Germany, 1922-1991)

- **Work:** co-founded the Ulm School of Design (1953). Braun graphics, the Lufthansa identity (1962-64), the Munich 1972 Olympics, the Rotis typeface (1988), the book *The World as Design*.
- **How he worked:** systematic, research-driven design over personal style. For Munich 1972: every pictogram drawn on one shared modular grid, one typeface (Univers) throughout, a palette led by light blue that deliberately left out red and black to distance the Games from Berlin 1936, all fixed in a 1969 design manual that a large in-house team applied from stadium signs to tickets.
- **For a logo:** write the rulebook (grid, typeface, colours, dos and don'ts) before the mark goes on anything.
- **Sources:** otlaicher.de/en/articles/the-rainbow-games/ · smithsonianmag.com/innovation/this-graphic-artists-olympic-pictograms-changed-urban-design-forever-180978256/ · eyemagazine.com/review/article/aichers-heroic-aspirations

### MUJI: Ikko Tanaka (1930-2002) and Kenya Hara (b. 1958)

- **Work:** Tanaka was MUJI's founding art director from its 1980 launch until his death (he also co-founded Nippon Design Center in 1960). Hara has been art director since the early 2000s (sources give 2001 and 2002), president of Nippon Design Center, author of *Designing Design* (2007) and *White* (2010).
- **How they work:** three founding rules: select the right materials, streamline the processes, simplify the packaging. No logo on the products; unbleached, undyed material is the colour; the form is deliberately plain ("empty") so customers complete it. Hara's research exhibitions (RE-DESIGN, 2000) feed ordinary products.
- **In Hara's words:** "Because it is empty, there's a possibility for it to be filled." (muji.eu)
- **For a logo:** let the product and the materials carry the brand; keep the applied mark small, quiet and consistent.
- **Sources:** muji.eu/pages/muji-stories/the-art-of-simple-design.html · hara.ndc.co.jp/en/about/ · dezeen.com/2017/12/13/kenya-hara-exclusive-interview-muji-us-expansion-brand-aesthetic/

## Identity studios

### Chermayeff & Geismar & Haviv (New York, founded 1957)

- **Work:** founded as Brownjohn, Chermayeff & Geismar; renamed in 2013 when Sagi Haviv (joined 2003) became partner. Chase (1960), Mobil (1964), NBC peacock (1986).
- **How they work:** idea before image, sketched by hand. They explore dozens of concepts, then the partners choose together. A mark is tested by shrinking it, applying it across surfaces and redrawing it from memory. Clients are advised as by experts, not run through focus groups. The aim is permanence.
- **In their words:** "A logo is not communication. It's identification." (Haviv, The Futur) · "We've never done a swoosh." (Geismar, PRINT)
- **For a logo:** one bold, simple form in one flat colour that holds at coin size and can be drawn from memory.
- **Sources:** cghnyc.com/about/sagi · logodesignlove.com/sagi-haviv-interview · printmag.com (interview "Marks men") · thefutur.com/content/what-makes-a-logo-great-with-sagi-haviv

### Pentagram and Michael Bierut (founded London 1972; Bierut a partner since 1990)

- **Work:** Pentagram was founded by Alan Fletcher, Colin Forbes, Theo Crosby, Kenneth Grange and Mervyn Kurlansky. Bierut: MIT Media Lab identity (2014), Mastercard simplification (2016).
- **How they work:** no CEO and no creative directors; the partners co-own the firm and each runs an independent team. Bierut distils a brand's existing assets in rounds of simplification with a small team, then tests the result from app icon to billboard. MIT's whole family of marks came from one grid.
- **In Bierut's words:** Mastercard was "a process of distillation" (Dezeen, 2016). "Each of us runs, on any typical day, what feels to us like our own studio." (Adweek)
- **For a logo:** simplify what the business already has (colours, name, sign) before inventing anything new; test every size.
- **Sources:** pentagram.com/about/michael-bierut · pentagram.com/work/mit-media-lab/story · dezeen.com/2016/07/15/pentagram-brings-mastercard-into-the-digital-age-with-pared-back-logo-redesign/ · adweek.com (Pentagram has no CEO or creative directors)

### Experimental Jetset (Amsterdam, founded 1997)

- **Work:** Marieke Stolk, Erwin Brinkers and Danny van den Dungen. "John & Paul & Ringo & George" T-shirt (2001), Whitney Museum identity (2013).
- **How they work:** three partners with no fixed roles who do everything together. The Whitney identity is a rule rather than a fixed logo: a zig-zag "W" line that redraws itself to fit any format, handed to the museum's team as a toolkit. Black and white with one grotesque typeface. (Their testing method is not documented: *unverified*.)
- **In their words:** "We find this whole notion of the 'graphic designer as a rock star' completely repulsive." (It's Nice That)
- **For a logo:** design one repeatable rule (a line, a dot, a crop) that the owner can apply consistently without you.
- **Sources:** jetset.nl/archive/whitney-museum-identity · designboom.com/design/experimental-jetset-interview/ · itsnicethat.com/features/experimental-jetset-interview-280616

### Build (Michael C. Place; London 2001, Leeds since 2015)

- **Work:** a studio of about four. Nike, Google, Getty Images, Virgin America.
- **How they work:** client call, scope, brief, team meeting, then designers work independently with the director overseeing. Modular, geometric marks, often on customised typefaces; a mark that works like a maker's stamp.
- **In their words:** "We go into each project without a preconceived idea of the outcome." (Creative Boom)
- **For a logo:** the mark must work alone, small, like a stamp on a cup or sign.
- **Sources:** studio.build/info · itsnicethat.com/features/lecture-in-progress-in-the-studio-with-build-200417 · creativeboom.com/inspiration/michael-c-place-of-studiobuild-/

### Spin (London, founded 1992 by Tony Brook and Patricia Finegan)

- **Work:** also co-founded the publisher Unit Editions (2009). Design Museum's Wim Crouwel exhibition (2011), BBC Creative (2018).
- **How they work:** reduce the brief to one rational idea, then give it maximum typographic impact. Brook makes things physically, with tape and builders' materials, before finishing on screen.
- **In their words:** "What's the clearest way of explaining my idea?" (Eye, *Reputations: Tony Brook*)
- **For a logo:** write the one sentence the mark must say, then find the plainest typographic form for it.
- **Sources:** spin.co.uk/about · eyemagazine.com/feature/article/reputations-tony-brook

### OK-RM (London, founded 2008 by Oliver Knight and Rory McGrath)

- **Work:** *Manus x Machina* identity for the Met (2016), *Fear and Love* at the Design Museum (2016).
- **How they work:** small and project-based; the team grows with artists and architects per brief. Dramatic empty space; modest means.
- **In their words:** "We identify a project's key point and exaggerate it to create magnified clarity." (Eye)
- **For a logo:** find the single truest fact about the business and magnify only that.
- **Sources:** ok-rm.co.uk · eyemagazine.com/feature/article/they-work-with-words-3

### Norm (Zurich, founded 1999 by Dimitri Bruni and Manuel Krebs)

- **Work:** books and typefaces, including Simple and Replica (2008).
- **How they work:** they restrict their own tools before starting (fewer grid lines, fixed parameters) and draw letterforms for the specific use. Craft, not self-expression.
- **In their words:** "We narrow the fields of what is possible." (Bruni, Eye) · "If you determine the field of work, you can create maximum effects with minimum means." (Krebs, Eye)
- **For a logo:** set one strict rule before drawing; the constraint becomes the identity.
- **Sources:** eyemagazine.com/feature/article/buying-into-the-norm-cosmos

### Order (Brooklyn, founded 2017 by Jesse Reed and Hamish Smyth)

- **Work:** ex-Pentagram founders; also Standards Manual (2014), which reissued the 1970 New York City Transit Authority graphics standards manual and NASA's 1970s manual. Herman Miller identity (2024), refining Irving Harper's "M".
- **How they work:** archival research first; systems rather than single logos; one typeface carries the voice; short guidelines published online with a printed companion.
- **For a logo:** one mark, one typeface, one colour rule, written on one page.
- **Sources:** order.design · order.design/project/herman-miller · standardsmanual.com/pages/about · the-brandidentity.com (interview with Jesse Reed)
